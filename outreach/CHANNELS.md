# Outreach channels: phone and contact forms

Status: proposal. Nothing here is built yet. Confirm the legal points with a lawyer before any phone or text outreach.

## Why this is needed

Many small businesses publish a phone number but no email address. The email pitch can't reach them, so we need another channel for that group.

## Options

| Channel | Reach | Legal risk | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email (current) | Only businesses with a published email | CAN-SPAM, plus stricter rules for UK and EU | Keep for businesses with email |
| Contact form on the business's own site | Businesses with a site, which we're not targeting | Form terms, and unsolicited-contact rules in some countries | Skip. This group already has a website. |
| Manual phone calls by a person | Businesses with a published phone number | Do-Not-Call rules, state laws, and call-recording consent | Possible, after legal review |
| Automated calls or texts (robocalls, autodialed texts) | Same | High. TCPA restricts autodialed calls and texts, including to business lines in some cases. Several states add stricter rules. | Do not build |

## What the research found

These points come from secondary sources, not the statutes. They need verification.

- **Do-Not-Call registry.** Business lines are generally not covered by the national registry, but exceptions exist, and the rules apply in full to some numbers, including mobile phones used for personal purposes.
- **Autodialers and texts.** The TCPA's autodialer and text rules don't have a clear business-to-business exemption, and some sources say they apply to mobile numbers.
- **State laws.** Some states have stricter rules with no business exemption. Florida is one example.
- **Internal do-not-call lists.** Even where the registry doesn't apply, a business that asks not to be called must be removed from our list.
- **Contact forms.** The results didn't address US rules for form submissions. In the UK and EU, unsolicited direct marketing generally requires prior consent.

## Proposed approach if we go ahead

1. **Manual calls only.** A person places each call, and no dialer automates them. This avoids the autodialer rules.
2. **Scripted, honest intro.** Identify the caller and company, state the purpose, and end the call if the person says no.
3. **Immediate opt-out.** Any request to stop calling marks the lead as unsubscribed and suppresses the number. The same suppression list applies to email.
4. **Call log.** Record each call's outcome in the same database as the email leads.
5. **Recording.** Don't record calls without the other party's consent, since state rules differ on one-party and two-party consent.
6. **Calling hours.** Restrict calls to reasonable local hours for the business.
7. **Registry check.** Scrub numbers against the national and state do-not-call lists before calling.

## Open questions for a lawyer

- Does the B2B exemption apply to our calls to business lines under the Telemarketing Sales Rule and state laws?
- Are manual calls to business lines covered by the TCPA when the number might be a personal mobile?
- Which states in NY and Long Island's customer area have stricter rules that apply to us?
- Is a contact form ever allowed, and what wording would it need?
- Is the 31-day scrub requirement and our record-keeping adequate?

## What's needed to decide

- Legal review of the questions above.
- A decision on whether the team will make the calls. Each call takes time, so the volume will be much lower than email.
